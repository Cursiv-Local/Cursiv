## 1. Give me a step-by-step plan to winterize a chicken coop.
*27.2 s*

Sure. Here’s a simple, step-by-step plan to winterize your chicken coop — focused on keeping it safe, dry, and warm for your chickens during cold weather.

1. **Inspect the roof and structure**  
Check for cracks, holes, or gaps in the roof or walls. Seal any with caulk or weatherproofing tape. Make sure the roof slopes properly to shed snow.

2. **Add insulation**  
Use breathable insulation (like straw or foam board) behind the walls and under the roof. Avoid using non-breathable materials that trap moisture.

3. **Improve ventilation**  
Keep a small gap or vent open (like a window or hatch) to allow airflow. Avoid drafts, but don’t block airflow completely — chickens need fresh air.

4. **Protect the floor**  
Lay a layer of clean straw or wood shavings on the floor. This helps absorb moisture and keeps the coop dry. Change it weekly or when wet.

5. **Keep the coop dry**  
Move the coop to a spot where it’s not directly under eaves or near drains. Avoid placing it in low-lying areas where snow melts and water pools.

6. **Provide extra heat (if needed)**  
If temperatures drop below 30°F (-1°C), consider a small, safe heat source like a ceramic heater (not electric space heaters). Never use open flames or heaters near bedding.

7. **Check regularly**  
Visit the coop every few days to check for leaks, ice buildup, or signs of freezing. Adjust insulation or airflow as needed.

This plan keeps your coop safe and your chickens healthy through winter.  

Note: If you live in a very cold climate (below 0°F), consult a local poultry expert or extension service — winterizing may need extra steps. Let me know if you'd like help with that.

## 9. Why did Einstein win the Nobel Prize for relativity?
*11.0 s*

Einstein did **not** win the Nobel Prize for relativity.

He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921 — but **not** for his theory of relativity (which includes special and general relativity).  

He won it for his explanation of the **photoelectric effect**, in which he showed that light can be treated as particles (called "photons") that carry energy. This work helped lay the foundation for quantum theory.

Relativity (especially special relativity) was a major breakthrough, but the Nobel Committee at the time recognized Einstein’s photoelectric effect work as the more directly measurable and impactful contribution — and it was supported by experiments that could be verified.

So:  
✅ Nobel Prize: 1921 — for the photoelectric effect  
❌ Not for relativity  

(Though relativity is one of his most famous achievements, the Nobel Prize was awarded for a different, earlier work.)
