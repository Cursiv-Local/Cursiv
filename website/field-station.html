Cursiv Field Station

A research station you can carry. A Raspberry Pi runs Cursiv and its own Wi-Fi; your phone, tablet or laptop is the screen. Record observations, check the notebook's integrity and watch the station's health with no internet at all.

The software is in Cursiv 3.14-U104 as a preview. Every screen below is real, running on a Windows PC with simulated hardware readings, each labelled SIMULATED. Nothing here has run on a Raspberry Pi yet; that comes when the hardware arrives, and results will be published as measured.

How it will work on the Pi

  1. Take the station out of its case and power it on.
  2. Join its Wi-Fi network from a phone, tablet or laptop. Each station will get its own password (the Wi-Fi setup is planned).
  3. Open Cursiv in the browser and sign in with a Cursiv account.
  4. Unlock the notebook with the encryption passphrase. After every restart it stays encrypted until someone does.
  5. Record observations, correct mistakes (the original is never edited), check integrity, and watch temperature, storage and power.
  6. Shut down from Settings before unplugging the battery.

Made for the phone in your hand

The interface is built for small screens first, works in any current browser, and needs nothing installed on the phone. It keeps working when the internet doesn't exist: the station never connects out.

Readings that never make things up

Every value on the Health page says where it came from: real from the hardware, derived from real readings, simulated in a test scenario, or unavailable. Old readings are marked stale.

Most USB-C power banks power a Raspberry Pi without telling it anything. Cursiv says exactly that: "Battery telemetry unavailable", never a percentage worked out from elapsed time or load. A battery board that reports its charge to Linux is read and labelled real.

Temperature comes from the board's own sensor, and throttling and undervoltage from the Pi's firmware flags. The warning levels (65, 75 and 80 °C) are advice you can change, not hardware limits.

The same trustworthy notebook

The station runs the same field notebook as Cursiv on Windows: append-only, each entry fingerprinted with the one before it, corrections added as new entries with a reason. One server process writes the notebook, so twenty people saving at once still produce one unbroken chain (tested). The integrity check runs without any AI model and says what a pass does and doesn't show.

Who can get in

  • Being on the Wi-Fi authorizes nothing: every page needs a Cursiv sign-in, and wrong passwords are slowed down.
  • The station only answers devices on its own private network, and checks every request came from its own page.
  • The first person to join can't make themselves administrator: setup needs a code shown only on the station itself.
  • The notebook is encrypted on the station's storage and locked after every restart.
  • No internet connections, ever, unless you turn them on.

Plain about the limits

The preview uses plain HTTP on the station's own Wi-Fi. The Wi-Fi password protects that traffic; the web page itself isn't encrypted, and the interface says so. Encrypted HTTPS without browser warnings needs more work and is planned as an option.

A Raspberry Pi is slower than a laptop. The notebook, checks, calculations and health readings don't need an AI model at all; local AI will be measured on the Pi before Cursiv recommends a model for it.

The kit being designed

A station that travels in one case

  • Raspberry Pi 58 or 16 GB, with active cooling
  • 512 GB external SSDfor the station's research data
  • USB workspaceseach a named, portable notebook, like team drives
  • Rechargeable batterytested for the Pi's power needs before it's recommended
  • Waterproof casefor transport and storage; the station runs out of the case

Nothing in the kit is for sale yet, and no battery is called compatible until it's been tested with a Pi 5.
