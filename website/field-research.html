For field researchers, students and monitoring teams

Field research software that keeps working when the signal doesn't

Cursiv is free, open-source desktop software for recording field observations, working out field calculations and keeping your records trustworthy. It runs on your own computer or from a USB drive. An optional local AI assistant can help you write and look things up, but your records and numbers never depend on it.

Download Cursiv for WindowsView the source

Windows is fully supported and physically tested. A Mac build is available but has only been tested in automated builds.

A tamper-evident field notebook

Record site, sample ID, coordinates, measurements with their units, notes and photos. Values are kept exactly as typed. Each entry stores a fingerprint of the one before it, so a later change, deletion or reordering shows up when you run the integrity check.

Mistakes are corrected by adding a new entry that points to the original, with a reason. The original is never edited. Exports (CSV, JSON, GeoJSON) are signed with the device's key, and any copy of Cursiv can check one with field log verify.

What a passing check shows, and what it doesn't, is spelled out in the app and in the notebook integrity results: 13 damage scenarios tested, from power cuts to edited files.

A team on field drives, one main computer

Each scientist gets a Cursiv drive made in their name and records offline on whatever PC is at hand. Back at base, one computer takes the drives one at a time: every entry and photo arrives in the name of the person who recorded it, a damaged copy is refused whole, and the main computer's own notebook is never replaced. A new drive can go out carrying only the notebooks you choose.

Field calculations done by code, not guessed

Conductivity corrected to 25 °C (APHA 2510 B), stream discharge with the surface-float correction, percent oxygen saturation (Benson & Krause, as in APHA 4500-O), dilutions, molar solutions, unit conversions and summary statistics are worked out by Cursiv's code and show their method. Impossible inputs, such as a temperature below absolute zero or a negative volume, are refused instead of answered.

A 237-case benchmark built from NIST unit definitions, APHA methods and IUPAC atomic weights runs on every change and passes 237 of 237. With the default local model, field calculations that needed a formula went from 9 of 14 right to 14 of 14 once Cursiv's code did the arithmetic. See the test results and their limits.

Ready-made helpers for field work

Five starter profiles come with Cursiv: @field for readings, @checker to find duplicate sample IDs and missing units before you submit data, @report to draft a report from your records, @protocol to answer from your own imported SOPs with page numbers, and @writer for posts and emails. They read only what they're allowed to and can't change anything.

Say things normally, such as "add a note that North Creek was 14.2 C". Cursiv shows exactly what it will save and waits for you to press Save.

Honest limits

Cursiv is in active development by an independent developer and has not been independently reviewed. The AI assistant can be wrong or too confident: check anything that matters against your records, which Cursiv keeps separate from what the model says. A tamper-evident chain shows records weren't changed after they were recorded; it doesn't prove an observation was correct.
