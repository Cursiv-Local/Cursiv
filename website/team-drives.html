Cursiv 3.14-U103 · real screens, sample data

Three field drives, one main computer

A research team works offline, each person on their own Cursiv drive. Back at base, one computer takes the drives one at a time, and every entry and photo arrives in the name of the person who recorded it. Then a new drive goes out carrying only the work someone chose.

1

Each scientist records on a drive made in their name

Ana's drive was made with "Whose drive is this? Ana Ruiz", so its notebook is hers on any PC she plugs it into. Each entry keeps measurements exactly as typed, a sample ID, notes and photos, and carries a fingerprint of the one before it. Cursiv keeps a team copy at the top of the drive, updated when it starts and quits.

2

The main computer takes the drives one at a time

On the field station, open the Field Notebook, choose Import from team… and pick the drive. Each person's log is checked before anything is kept: the whole chain, the signature from that drive's own key, and every photo against its fingerprint. A damaged copy is refused whole, never half-imported, and the main computer's own notebook is never replaced.

3

Everyone's work, side by side, in their names

Type team log in the chat for the whole team, or team log export for CSV, JSON and GeoJSON files. Each entry says whose notebook it came from, and so does each photo, so nobody's work is shown under someone else's name.

4

A new drive goes out with only the work chosen

Making Dee Park's drive, the field station ticks Ana's and Cara's notebooks and leaves Ben's and its own unticked. Nothing is included unless it's ticked. Dee's drive gets its own empty notebook and its own signing key; the main computer's notebook isn't copied onto it.

5

On Dee's drive: Ana's and Cara's work, nothing else

In the field, Dee can read the two notebooks she was given, still in their authors' names, with their photos. Ben's work and the field station's own notebook aren't on the drive at all.

Checked on every change

Tested before every release, on Windows, macOS and Linux

  • 20 drives with 1,200 entries and their photos arrive byte for byte, each in the right name.
  • Six kinds of damage on a drive (an edited entry, a cut-off copy, a missing or altered manifest, garbage, a damaged photo) are caught. A damaged log is refused whole, and a damaged photo is named, never kept.
  • A drive pulled out mid-import leaves nothing half-written; plugging it in again finishes the job.
  • Bringing the same drives in again changes nothing on disk.
  • A log can only hold its own notebook's entries, so one drive's key can't put work under someone else's name.
  • A new drive carries only the notebooks ticked, and re-making it keeps the same person's notebook.

The tests are tests/test_team_drives.py in the mandatory USB checks; results for each release are on the testing page.

Read that answer closely

It also says oxygen saturation at 10 °C is "about 12.5–13.0 mg/L". The standard equation gives 11.29, so a reading of 12.5 means the water is supersaturated, not "normal". Cursiv's saturation check missed it because the figure was given as a range; making this page found that, and the fix is in the next update. The line under the answer is a prompt to check, not a guarantee: on 50 test questions the local model settled a verdict in 7 answers, and the check is still being measured on new questions and other models.

Limits today

What to know before relying on it

  • Team drives are tested automatically; a physical test with real drives hasn't been done yet.
  • Nothing is encrypted per person: anyone signed in on the main computer can read the team's work. A drive's notebook and signing key are readable by anyone holding the drive.
  • A drive used on two different PCs before U103 is refused at import; drives made since keep one label everywhere.
  • If a drive is pulled while Cursiv is open, the last few entries arrive on its next start.
