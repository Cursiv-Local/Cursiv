No signal, no problem

Offline scientific data collection

Remote sites, ships, caves and classrooms without Wi-Fi: Cursiv's field notebook, field calculations, imported protocols and local AI assistant all run on your computer after setup. Offline mode stops Cursiv itself from connecting to anything outside the computer, and stays on until you turn it off.

Works offline after setup

  • Field notebookEntries, photos, corrections, integrity checks, signed exports.
  • Field calculationsConductivity, discharge, oxygen saturation, dilutions, units, statistics, worked out by code.
  • Your documentsImported protocols, SOPs and permits, quoted with page numbers.
  • Local AI chatWith Ollama and a compatible model installed. Answers come from your computer.
  • Memory and conversationsStored on the computer, encrypted on disk.

Needs a connection

Installing Ollama and downloading a model, web search, checking for updates, optional online AI providers and the website-based phone app's first load. When you turn one on and use it, the content it needs goes to that service; offline mode blocks all of them.

Offline mode is enforced in Cursiv's code (a network gate), not by turning off Windows networking, and it can't control other software on the computer. The offline demonstration records a run with no outside connection attempts; it is evidence for that procedure, not a certification.

Why offline matters for research data

Field records are most at risk where connectivity is worst. Cursiv saves every notebook entry to the computer before it reports it saved, reads it back to confirm, and keeps the record chain so damage from a power cut or a bad copy is reported rather than hidden. Your data doesn't wait for a sync to exist. When you're back online, nothing is uploaded unless you choose a feature that does.
